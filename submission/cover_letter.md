We submit our review paper, "Autonomy Modes and Validation Depth in Disaster Search-and-Rescue Robotics: A Systematic Review of the 2025–2026 Literature", for consideration in the *Journal of Field Robotics*.

The *Journal of Field Robotics* encourages comparative and meta-studies and the validation of autonomous systems in representative environments. This review addresses both. It classifies 39 disaster search-and-rescue (SAR) robotics studies published in 2025–2026 into four autonomy/human-robot interaction modes, grounded in levels-of-automation theory, and cross-tabulates those modes against how each study was validated. The main findings are as follows:

- Fully Autonomous systems make up 69% of the recent literature.
- Most of these systems were evaluated in simulation, and none included a human in the evaluated mission.
- Only 15% of all studies tested a physical system at a real site.
- Human-in-the-loop systems were field-tested more often. This difference is consistent in direction but not statistically established in a corpus of this size, and we report it as such.
- A post hoc per-function analysis shows that the modes differ in who holds decisions and actions, while perception is automated almost everywhere.

Several recent reviews are close to this work. Kareem et al. (2026, *IEEE Access*) proposed a five-layer architecture for 2016–2024 SAR robotics. Sinha et al. (2026, *Sensors*) reported how rarely ground robots reach field deployment. Shuvo et al. (2026, *Frontiers in Robotics and AI*) organized SAR research by learning paradigm. Bakzadeh et al. (2026, *Frontiers in Robotics and AI*) coded mine-rescue platforms on a three-band autonomy scale. To our knowledge, none classifies a research corpus primarily by human decision authority or relates that classification to validation depth. That question bears directly on how the field's growing emphasis on autonomy should be read.

We have aimed to report exactly what was done:

- **Identification.** Records were identified by one author through publisher-platform searches, Google Scholar, and AI-assisted discovery (ChatGPT and Gemini). The two routes are reported separately in the PRISMA diagram.
- **Screening and extraction.** Two authors screened all records. Data extraction and drafting were assisted by an AI system.
- **Disclosure.** All AI use is disclosed in the AI-use statement, in line with Wiley's policy.
- **Limitations.** The manuscript states the limitations that follow from these choices. It also reports strict and lenient codings, Holm-adjusted tests, and five sensitivity analyses.
- **Data.** The full screening log, the page-referenced extraction table, the coding manual, the PRISMA 2020 and PRISMA-S checklists, and the analysis scripts are provided as supporting information. [[AUTHOR INPUT NEEDED: repository DOI]]
